    How to read this
    • Coloured columns run from the ground to the usable ceiling. Colour is thermal strength (W*), a guide to climb rates rather than a measured value.
    • A white line marks cloudbase on hours when cumulus should form. Where there is no white line the ceiling is the top of the thermals, so expect blue conditions.
    • Grey shading shows the model's cloud cover at each height, with a cloud icon where a layer reaches 40% or more. Cumulus is shaded from cloudbase up to the thermal top.
    • The Wave strip is a rough guide. It darkens when the wind aloft is strong, increases with height, holds a steady direction and meets a stable layer. These are ingredients for wave, not a forecast of it, because the free models cannot resolve the lift itself.
    • NOTAMs come from the NATS UK pre-flight bulletin, which can be an hour out of date. A notice is drawn as its written boundary when it has one, and otherwise as a circle from its centre point and radius. Smaller areas sit on top of larger ones. Notices are filtered by their start and end dates and by schedules such as FRI SAT 1400-2300 (UTC). A schedule in any other form is not read, so the notice is shown and tagged.
    • "Starts below forecast ceiling" means the notice's lower limit is under the highest thermal ceiling the model forecasts for the date and time you chose. It is not a fixed height and says nothing about wave.
    • Map tiles come from OpenStreetMap and need an internet connection. Google's map tiles cannot be used outside Google's own map code, and CARTO's need an API key.
    • Heights are above mean sea level. Barbs show wind from the direction of the staff, with full barbs for 10 kt and half barbs for 5 kt.
    • Thermal top comes from lifting a surface parcel, warmed according to sunshine, up through the model's pressure-level temperatures. Cloudbase uses 125 m per °C of dew point spread. For hill sites enter the field elevation so surface values are corrected.
    • Data is from Open-Meteo, which is free for non-commercial use and serves ECMWF, ICON, GFS and UKMO output. Upper-air data differs by model, so try another if the columns look empty. This is not a substitute for an official briefing.
